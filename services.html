The Difference

Surveying is in the foundation

I'm a licensed surveyor. That shapes how I think about data: what accurate actually means, and why it matters downstream.

Surveying trains you to care about things that aren't always visible in the output: coordinate systems, control networks, accuracy documentation. The kind of details that seem invisible until an engineer tries to use the data six months later and needs it to hold up.

I do topographic survey, spatial control, and QA/QC on remote sensing deliverables because that foundation matters. Not as a credential to display, but because it changes what I notice and what I check.

GNSS & Project Control Deep GNSS experience: benchmarks, control networks, and the consistent spatial framework that lets LiDAR, GIS, and construction all reference the same ground. I lead industry workshops on this because it's genuinely interesting work.
Coordinate Systems Done Right The right datum, projection, and coordinate system from the start, so everything integrates cleanly and nothing has to be untangled later.
Collected with the End in Mind Knowing how terrain data gets used downstream in design, analysis, and construction shapes how I collect it. What gets captured and how it gets documented isn't an afterthought.
