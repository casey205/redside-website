The Background

Grounded in Surveying. Driven by Terrain.

I started Redside because I kept finding myself at the overlap of two things I genuinely care about: precise spatial data and the landscapes that data describes.

My background is in land surveying, and that's not incidental to what Redside does. It's the foundation. Surveying teaches you to be honest about accuracy, careful about coordinate systems, and clear-eyed about the difference between data that looks right and data you can actually build on. That distinction shapes every project I take on.

GNSS is where I've spent a lot of focused time: field workflows, project control, benchmark networks, the kind of consistent spatial framework across a site that lets LiDAR, GIS, and construction data all reference the same thing. I've led industry workshops on GNSS practice and find that work genuinely interesting. Helping teams get reliable results in demanding terrain is a real problem worth solving.

Bend is home base. The work takes me across the Pacific Northwest, and the projects I find most interesting tend to involve complex terrain and people doing meaningful work in it. Trails, rivers, restoration sites, places where the quality of the spatial data actually changes what's possible.

Reality meshes, custom analytical workflows, interactive delivery as a standard part of how projects get communicated — this is where the industry is going. Most of my clients are encountering these tools for the first time. Part of what I do is help them understand what's possible and get real value from it, not just hand off a file and move on.
